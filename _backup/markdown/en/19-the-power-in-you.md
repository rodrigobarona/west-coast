# #19 The power in you

![img_7747](../../media/RNeHm5lJQL-s8bHnBmNd4g-1717784572-img_7747.jpeg)

After a lot of thought, and contacting the respective parts of our professional anchors in Portugal, we are going to extend our stay by another year. When we presented the proposal to the family team, the reaction was immediate.

May was by far the busiest month of activities since we arrived here.

Decisions and authorizations about the future, art festivals, state testings, the final stretch of the school year, rehearsals for all kinds of end-of-year shows, tryouts for the sports of the following years, girl scout camps, information sessions about the schools they'll be going to next year, awards nights, graduations/promotions/whatever the ceremony is called \(the 3 of them change schools and celebrate the end of the cycle\), end-of-year loop parties, and the start of a new work project. And visits that warm your heart... and I've been more emotional this month than during my entire stay... 

After a lot of thought, and contacting the respective parts of our professional anchors in Portugal, we are going to extend our stay by another year. When we presented the proposal to the family team, the reaction was immediate.

We want to stay. Without hesitation.

It's still scary, and we warned them that the project has been extended for another year, and that August 2025 is the end of the project, after which, if everything continues as it is, we'll return to our west coast in Europe. And the sisters argued that that's fine, but if we have another year to continue getting to know this coast and this community, and everything we're entitled to, let's make the most of it and return with our luggage fuller of the world.

It's inevitable to compare the reactions of the sisters with their attitude a year ago, expectant but full of uncertainty.

When we set out on this adventure, the thing that frightened both of us the most was failure.

In school, in tests, challenges, and adapting to change. They said things like, what if I don't get good grades? What if I don't have any friends? What if I can't explain what I need to and I'm alone? What if I get lost and don't know where we live? What if ...

And we tried, badly or not, to take the weight off our fears. We even said, if you don't achieve the expected results and we go back to Portugal and you have to repeat the year, that's okay! What we're going to experience and what we're going to learn can't be learned in books!

But this conversation didn't go well because we distressed them even more...

Returning to Portugal and having to do it all over again, and repeat the year...!

But is everything crazy? I don't want that!

Me neither!

Crying and tears aside, we changed tack immediately to reassure them that we were sure that wouldn't happen, that we would all try our best, and that anyone who needed help had to immediately ask for all kinds of help at home and school, and that we promised not to disregard their fears and to take it easy when we tried to put it into perspective so as not to upset them any further, and always to be available. Even if we didn't sleep. When they got home, we could help them with whatever they needed from school.

And so it was.

I returned to this photo of nervous smiles at the start of this year a few days ago...


![img_8643](../../media/66443706-1692288381-img_8643.jpeg)
The 3 siblings were true ambassadors and intermediaries of the most delightful international relationships, and I'm absolutely sure that despite all the stumbles and bumps in the road since we first thought of coming, the Universe was kind to have put the people we met during this year on our paths, from work colleagues, students, friends, teachers, and staff at the 3 different schools, neighbors, coaches, in short, the whole community we landed in was facilitating this huge process.

May provide an opportunity to see and celebrate all of this.

We started by attending the arts evening at Maria's school.

She told us that we would see the choir to which she belongs and that she had gotten a solo.

I've tried to apologize several times because although honesty is one of the things I've raised my children to value when Maria asked me if I thought it was right for her to audition for a solo, I asked her if it was to dance or to sing.

When she explained to me that it was to sing, I tried to talk her out of it by making the analogy with two words that I regret: "parrot in agony."

I'm sure that when I tell my children that the results of their work are incredible, they'll believe me because I also use "parrots in agony" to explain what I think of some of their projects.

Angry Maria rehearsed independently, auditioned, and got a solo line. And I swallowed the unconstructive feedback I gave her. In addition to the artistic demonstration of everything done during the year, the choir sang that evening. Maria shone and looked nothing like a parrot, and then they offered the flower they had chosen to someone special.

Amina, whom we bumped into on the day of the locker set-up, who waited for Maria for lunch on the first day of school, who taught her a series of cool expressions and local flavors, who guided her in choosing the company to be with, who introduced her to a series of impeccable girls, and who integrated her into the Girl Scouts group, received the flower.

And Maria told her, "thank you for making my life so much easier at school this year. You are an amazing friend." 


![img_2180](../../media/HqniHon_S9WlbAXpXMNMCg-1717899411-img_2180.jpeg)

Pamela, the mother of this incredible girl and who welcomed Maria into the Girl Scouts, where she is a leader, can only be proud of the genuine generosity that her teenage daughter showed Maria.

To round off this year's Girl Scouts program, we held a mother-daughter camp at Camp Sherman with various team-building activities, sports, hikes, and a symbolic transition to seniors. Everything was prepared with individualized messages to each of them and plenty of opportunities to watch friends interact.

I loved it.
