# #26 Leaving California

![img_7326](../../media/FFZqULT5QlKrCUz9oa3nRg-1753076760-img_7326.jpeg)

I'm sitting in the middle of the chaos.  Our house doesn't feel like home anymore. 

I'm sitting in the middle of the chaos.

Our house no longer feels like home. There are a lot of suitcases and a lot of closed boxes, and so many loose ends that it looks like a camp of disorganized people. Falling asleep in the middle of this happens because we're completely melted. With just over 24 hours to go before we take off, we feel like we're watching the same movie from two years ago, but in reverse. And it's so hard and so beautiful that each one of us have cried our hearts out, and our hearts have been filled with the best of the daily record.

Before we reached this stage of saying goodbye, we had spent the last two months in a mix of intense professional, school, and sporting activities, fitting in all the events and programs with friends, and fulfilling the bucket list that we had prepared before leaving.

Despite my apprehension about the consequences of protests, I have to confess that the fact that they've started in Los Angeles makes me a little proud, and is in line with the bewilderment in this state and in our hearts. 

I confess that when I'm less into globalization, I think that if California were a country, it would not only be a world power in every sense, but also the country with the coolest philosophy of life, easy vibes, fantastic climate, and the most \(very!\) diverse paradises I've ever known. I like this place and this community where we landed two years ago.

In Seal Beach, there were also protests where we met some of our people. Great Seal Beach. Not capitulation.

I've already realized that it's wonderful to be back.

I've realized that it's painful not to be in Seal Beach.

In an early morning conversation with Pamela \(jet lag and insomnia be damned\), a wet swimsuit came to mind.  Few things compare to riding fresh waves in the sea, and we spend the whole year counting down the days to summer. After the beach, wet swimsuits, sand, and salt are anything but comfortable. Being at home, safe, without having to justify obvious premises in visa applications, enjoying so many Portuguese wonders and hugs is indescribable. At the end of the day, not returning to Seal Beach is also hard to describe. It's as uncomfortable as a wet bathing suit at the end of a wonderful summer's day.

Mercedes asked \(as always!\) if we belong a little bit to California, or if we're just from here. Whether the fever and vomiting she had could be nerves from leaving, or whether it was some bug that had hurt her. Whether we'll remember everything, whether they'll remember us, and when we'll return to Seal Beach. I've been telling them that if we want to calm our hearts and not be stuck in limbo between being uprooted and wondering where our roots are, we can decide that we're citizens of the world, and that's fine! We can be from various places or have the places in us. Given the amount of time they've lived in Seal Beach, especially at such a young age, being Portuguese, with a Spanish rib, and traces of California is apparently the most reassuring thing for them at the moment. 

The heart hasn't calmed down yet. The cultural shock of readaptation still seems to be setting in. For now, the most shocking thing is the scary driving style and the ridiculously small parking spaces. I've thought we were going to have accidents 327 times since we arrived, and I've thought my car won't fit anywhere a few times.

From someone who has been through this rough and tumble with family comes the warmth: "It's hard, but you get through it. Accept the change, keep an open mind, and try not to compare. Appreciate the good things. If we can get to Lisbon before school starts, we'll hug each other." Thanks, Mónica. If not, we'll go to Madrid, which suddenly feels like a short 5-hour drive. It's a promise.

I'm certain that we're still going to have a pilgrimage ahead of us, with everyone having to adapt to everything again, and that some things can be adapted to immediately, others not so much. In any case, I also know that we'll get through whatever we have to. As always.

Liking California so much is not synonymous with liking Portugal or Spain less. This is clear to us, but sometimes it doesn't seem clear in our sharing. Several places fit in our hearts... we went at the right time, because we wanted to. We came back \(it seems to me\) at the right time, because we also decided to. And we want to return to Seal Beach as often as possible. And we also really want to show off the wonders of our little corner to the most impeccable Californians who are our friends.

To calm Mercedes down, I told her that a bit of California belongs to us \(what a luxury!\) and it will last forever. There are more difficult places to be a visiting scholar, I once heard at a meeting.

And now I know there are easier places to get out of. Stay strong, heart!
